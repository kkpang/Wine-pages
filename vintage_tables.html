Why climate sits in its own column

Rating and climate are genuinely independent, and the page now shows it. 2021 is a lesser year of cool character; 2018 is a better year of hot character. Which is the better bottle depends on the drinker, and that is the whole job of palate fit.

The fit column is not a quality judgement

Red prefers cool and avoids hot; white prefers classic and warm, and avoids cool and hot. So the same year scores differently in the red and white tables — a hot year is −2 in both, but a cool one is +2 for red and 0 for white. The endpoints are ±2, half the style ladder's ±4, and like it these are values at the anchor: the cross-term shrinks them on wine that was already going to be very good. Red's middle two rungs are an interpolation rather than Ken's own words.

This page is now built, not written

Everything above is generated from vintages.md by tools/build_vintage_tables.py. Between 2026-08-11 and today it was hand-authored, and it went stale twice without anything noticing. Re-run the builder after any rating change and republish — the same standing rule as the dashboard and the tier ladders.

One column of the old page did not survive: src, the direct-plus-bundled source counts. They were worked out by hand from material that was never committed, so nothing in the project can regenerate or check them. The thinly sourced marker, which vintages.md does record, carries what is left of that signal.
