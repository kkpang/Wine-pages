Trapet · Latricières-Chambertin

Eleven vintages probed, nine priced. The cheaper neighbour to Trapet's Chambertin — and on this evidence the better buy.

Latricières sits directly above Chambertin on the slope, makes a lighter and higher-toned wine, and carries a cru level one rung below it — so it is cheaper by construction. What makes it the better buy here is that Trapet's gentle style wins outright on this cru instead of on a tie-break: the wine resolves to Delicate & Perfumed at 42%, where the Chambertin only managed a 35/35 draw. You get more of what you like for a little over half the money. The 2016 is a classic-weather great+ year, it is the only cheap row here sold as a proper case, and the next case on the market sits 20% above it. Avoid the hot years — 2018 and 2020 both turn the palate fit negative, and 2015 is thin on offers as well.

The finding worth more than the buy. Across the nine vintages both wines share, the market prices Latricières at 0.58× Trapet's Chambertin. The framework's cru levels — S− against S+ — predict 0.58×. That is an exact match on a comparison that holds producer, winemaking and vintage constant, which makes it the cleanest test of a cru level the project has run.

In the glass

Look

Lighter than the Chambertin from the same cellar, and lighter than most grand cru Gevrey — pale ruby, translucent at the rim even when young.

⚠ Inferred from site and house style; critics rarely describe colour.

Nose

Higher-toned than Chambertin — red cherry and rose rather than black fruit, with a cool mineral edge that comes from sitting further up the slope on thinner soil. The Gevrey earth is there but further back.

Palate

Fresher and more linear. The framework calls the site Pure & Bright as its second style and that is the difference you taste: less flesh than Chambertin, more lift, and tannins that are firm but fine. It drinks earlier.

Palate fit

This is the cru where Trapet's style actually lands. On Chambertin the producer and the site tie at 35% each and the gentle reading wins only on the tie-break. Here the site pulls less hard — it is a rung lower and its second style is Pure & Bright rather than Rich & Silky — so the wine resolves to Delicate & Perfumed at 42% outright. That is your best-liked style, arrived at without an asterisk.

And the vintage still moves it more than the wine does. Cool years gain heavily: 2014 and 2021 both take +3.2, the largest palate-fit swings on either Trapet ladder. Hot years give back 0.3–0.4. The spread from best to worst fit here is 3.6 points, against 2.8 on the Chambertin — so choosing the right year matters more on this cru, not less.

Nothing in your tasting log bears on this wine. The one Trapet entry is a Louis Trapet Chambertin 1981 from a restaurant — different producer, different cru, forty years old, one night. It argues nothing here.

What to buy

1 · 2016 — the case

6×75cl · HK$10,350 · HK$1,725/btl · Cru World Wine Hong Kong

For — 33% under the model on quality and 53% under on your taste, the biggest combined discount on the ladder. A great+ classic-weather year, fit +1.9, and the next case sits 20% above it. Eleven offers with eight cases behind it, so the price is real and repeatable.

Against — 2016 was the frost year; the crop was tiny and bottles are finite. Drink 2030–2040, so it is a cellaring buy, not a drinking one.

2 · 2021 — the cheap cool year

1×75cl · HK$1,800 · Cru World Wine Hong Kong

For — the joint-best palate fit on the ladder (+3.2, a cool year) and 45% under the model against your taste. Deepest market here: 18 offers, 13 cases.

Against — the case market is 85% above this loose bottle, so the HK$1,800 is not a case price and the market value behind it rests on two loose offers. Treat it as unsettled. JM 93 is the lowest gate score on the ladder, and 2021 was a frost-hit, light year.

3 · 2017 — the cheapest bottle

1×75cl · HK$1,680 · Cru World Wine Hong Kong

For — the lowest price on either Trapet ladder, 19% under the model on quality and 35% under on taste, fit +1.3. Charming, early-maturing year that suits the house. Twelve offers, six cases.

Against — a single bottle again, with cases only 7% behind, so the saving over a case is small. A very good+ year rather than a great one.

What the ladder says, and what to distrust

The model tracks this market, though less tightly than on the Chambertin. r = +0.60 across the nine priced years, against +0.70 for the bigger wine. The market's spread (2.04×) is again wider than the model's (1.55×), which is the safe direction. The percentages are usable; the individual rows are noisier than on the Chambertin because several rest on very few offers.

This is a split market and the old end is simply gone. 2010 has a market value but no buyable offer at all; 2013 has one offer in the world and prices at neither. 2014 has a price but too few cases to compute a market value, and 2015 has three offers total. From 2016 the market is normal — 11 to 18 offers a year. Trapet makes roughly a third as much Latricières as Chambertin and it is the less famous name, so back vintages get drunk rather than traded. 2011 and 2012 were not fetched — the 2010 and 2013 probes bracketed them as dead and the two paced page loads were not worth spending to confirm it.

Unusually, almost every row here is cheaper than the next case behind it — eight of nine are positive on that measure, where the Chambertin had eight of thirteen going the other way. That is the opposite of the usual pattern and it says the cheapest-buyable offers on this wine are genuinely the cheap ones, not artefacts of the Hong-Kong-first rule.

What to distrust. The 2020 and 2021 market values rest on two loose offers each, with their case pools running 50% and 85% above — those two rows are the least settled on the page and the 2021 is my second pick, so weigh it accordingly. The 2019 looks expensive (33% over the model) and it is the one row where Latricières costs nearly as much as the Chambertin (0.88× against a 0.58× norm) — either that offer is dear or the vintage is genuinely scarce here; one row cannot tell you which. And the critic column is four-ninths empty for the same sourcing reason as the Chambertin report: Cru's product pages are unreachable without an exact slug, so only the vintages where wine-searcher named a gate critic are filled. Off-gate scores exist on every row and are deliberately not shown.
